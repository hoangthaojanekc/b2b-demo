Marketing → sales data

Making the handoff usable

Every lead starts in marketing and has to arrive clean in sales — and deal status has to flow back so nurture keeps working. The goal isn't a particular tool; it's data both teams trust. Which path fits depends on the state of the data today — and choosing well is part of the job.

Option A

Buy the native connector

The two platforms talk directly, through a paid off-the-shelf link.

What we get

  • Fastest to stand up — mostly clicking, not building.
  • Vendor maintains it; little ongoing engineering.
  • Predictable for a simple, single-owner setup.

Works best when

  • The data is already clean and low-volume.
  • Speed to launch matters more than deep control.
  • One team owns both systems and agrees on the fields.
  • The paid tier is already in budget.
Option B

Own the bridge in-house

Both platforms route through our own warehouse — where we clean and match the data, then build the analysis on the same trusted foundation.

What we get

  • Matching we control — duplicates caught before they reach sales.
  • We choose exactly what syncs, and in which direction.
  • Every sync attempt logged and queryable — real visibility.
  • Dashboards, attribution and funnel built on the same clean data.

Works best when

  • Duplicates or mismatches are already causing friction.
  • Marketing and sales disagree on the numbers.
  • We want attribution and budget analysis on data we trust.
  • Volume or complexity makes owning it worth the effort.

THE POINT

The right answer depends on the state of the data — and deciding is part of the role. Either way the outcome is the same: data both teams can act on, and an end to the duplicate-and-mismatch frustration that stalls marketing and sales.
